Chapter 7

The Final Wish

Aladdin defeats Jafar by turning his hunger for power against him, then uses his final wish to free the Genie.

Determined to save Jasmine and free Agrabah from Jafar's rule, Aladdin returned to the palace and confronted the sorcerer before the people he now controlled through fear and magic. Although Jafar possessed enormous power, Aladdin understood something his enemy did not. Throughout his life, he had survived by observing people carefully, and he recognised that Jafar's greatest weakness was not a lack of strength but an endless hunger for more of it.

As the confrontation unfolded, Aladdin deliberately challenged Jafar's pride. He argued that despite all his magical abilities, Jafar was still not the most powerful being in the room. That title, he claimed, belonged to the Genie. The suggestion immediately struck a nerve. Jafar had spent his entire life seeking greater authority, and the idea that someone else remained more powerful than him was impossible for his pride to accept.

Blinded by ambition, Jafar made a final and disastrous decision. He wished to become an all-powerful genie, believing that such a transformation would grant him unlimited control over the world. For a brief moment, it appeared that his wish had succeeded. Magical energy erupted throughout the palace as his powers expanded beyond anything he had previously possessed.

However, Jafar had overlooked a crucial detail.

A genie's immense power came at a cost.

The same magic that granted his wish also bound him to a lamp, just as it had bound the Genie for centuries. Golden shackles appeared around his wrists, and a black lamp materialised beside him. Before he could understand what was happening, his body dissolved into swirling red smoke and was pulled into the lamp. The most powerful sorcerer in the world had become a prisoner of his own ambition.

With Jafar defeated, peace gradually returned to Agrabah. The Sultan reclaimed his throne, Jasmine was freed from captivity, and the fear that had spread throughout the kingdom began to fade. Although Aladdin still possessed one final wish, he chose not to use it for wealth, power, or status. Instead, he used it to grant the Genie the freedom he had been denied for centuries.

The selfless act changed everything. Impressed by Aladdin's honesty, courage, and willingness to put others before himself, the Sultan abolished the ancient law requiring Jasmine to marry a prince. Free to choose her own future, Jasmine chose Aladdin. As celebrations filled the city and the Genie set off to explore the world as a free being, Agrabah entered a new chapter built not upon status or power, but upon trust, fairness, and the freedom to choose one's own path.
